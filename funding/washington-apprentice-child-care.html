Child care and support services for Washington apprentices

Working Connections Child Care (WCCC)

WCCC is the state child-care subsidy run by the Department of Children, Youth, and Families (DCYF). Normally, household income has to be at or below 60% of state median income (SMI) when you first apply. Apprentices get a wider limit:

  • In the first 12 months of a state-registered apprenticeship, household income adjusted for family size can be up to 75% of SMI.
  • Children must be under 13, or under 19 with a verified special need or under court supervision.
  • You must live in Washington, have parental control of the child, have assets under $1,000,000, and meet DCYF's other rules.
  • Depending on income, you may owe your provider a monthly copayment.

Apply through DCYF. Check its site for current income tables.

Wraparound support grants (RCW 49.04.270)

State law sets up a grant program, managed by the Department of Labor & Industries (L&I), to remove barriers to starting or staying in a state-registered apprenticeship. It pays for support services such as child care, health care, transportation to jobsites, professional clothing and required tools. The grants go to nonprofits and apprenticeship training committees, which then help apprentices. Funding depends on legislative appropriations. Ask your apprenticeship committee or L&I which organizations currently offer help.
